Lesson 1

Understanding the Product Manager Role

Product managers play a crucial role in shaping the direction and success of digital products. By understanding what a product manager does, you’ll build a strong foundation for all other product management skills. This lesson will help you see how the PM role connects teams, drives outcomes, and ensures products deliver real value to users.

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  1. Describe the core responsibilities of a product manager.
  2. Identify the stages of the product lifecycle.
  3. Distinguish between the roles of PM, project manager, and product owner.
  4. Recognize essential skills for effective product managers.

Welcome to the Course

Welcome to Introduction to Product Management! Throughout this course, you’ll follow the story of Mwangaza, a learning app designed for Kenyan secondary students. By exploring real-world scenarios from Mwangaza’s journey, you’ll see how product management concepts apply in practice and gain insights you can use in your own career.

Meet the Mwangaza Team

Get to know the five key members of the Mwangaza team. Each plays a unique role in building and improving the product. You’ll see how they work together throughout the course.

Expand each panel to learn about their responsibilities and how they contribute to the product process.

The PM leads the team to ensure they build the right features for the right users at the right time. They balance user needs, business goals, and technical constraints.

The designer creates user-friendly interfaces and experiences. They turn ideas into wireframes and prototypes, focusing on how students interact with the app.

The engineer builds and maintains the app’s technical foundation. They turn designs and requirements into working software, ensuring reliability and performance.

The data analyst tracks how students use the app and uncovers insights from usage data. They help the team make evidence-based decisions.

The sponsor provides resources and strategic direction. They ensure the team’s work aligns with the organization’s goals and supports long-term success.

A product manager makes sure the team builds the right thing, for the right people, at the right time.

Product managers focus on outcomes, not just outputs. Instead of simply delivering features, they aim to solve real problems for users and the business.

By prioritizing outcomes, PMs help teams avoid wasted effort and ensure every release delivers value. This means asking, "What impact will this have?" rather than just, "What did we build?"

Reflect: How might focusing on outcomes over outputs change the way you approach your own work?

What Do Product Managers Do Day to Day?

Explore the main activities that fill a product manager’s daily schedule. Each responsibility is essential to guiding the team and delivering value. Expand each section to see how these tasks play out at Mwangaza.

Review each panel for a real-world example.

PMs identify user needs and pain points by talking to students and teachers. At Mwangaza, the team noticed students struggled to find practice quizzes, prompting further investigation.

They weigh different opportunities and decide which problems to tackle first. For example, Mwangaza’s PM prioritized improving quiz discoverability over adding new features.

PMs work with designers and engineers to brainstorm and select the best solutions. At Mwangaza, this meant testing different layouts to make quizzes easier to find.

They coordinate the team’s work, ensuring everyone is aligned and obstacles are removed. The PM at Mwangaza led sprint planning and checked in daily to keep progress on track.

After launch, PMs track usage and feedback to see if the solution worked. Mwangaza’s PM reviewed analytics to confirm more students were completing quizzes.

Introduction

Every product moves through a series of stages, from initial idea to eventual retirement. Understanding these stages helps PMs guide products to success.

Idea

This is where a new product or feature is first imagined, often sparked by a user need or business goal.

Validate

The team tests assumptions and gathers evidence to confirm there’s real demand before investing heavily in development.

Build

Designers and engineers work together to create the product or feature, guided by the PM’s vision and requirements.

Launch

The product goes live for users. The team monitors for issues and gathers early feedback.

Grow

As the product gains traction, the team adds improvements, scales usage, and optimizes performance.

Sunset

Eventually, some products or features are retired when they’re no longer needed or valuable. PMs plan for a smooth transition.

Summary

By understanding the product lifecycle, PMs can make better decisions at every stage and ensure the product delivers lasting value.

Comparing PM, Project Manager, and Product Owner

Product manager, project manager, and product owner are three roles that often overlap but have distinct responsibilities. Explore each tab to see how they differ in focus, key questions, measures of success, and typical artefacts.

Expand each tab for a real-world example of each role in action.

Focus: Product vision and user value
Key question: What should we build and why?
Success measure: User and business outcomes
Typical artefact: Product roadmap

Example: The PM at Mwangaza decides to prioritize a new quiz feature based on student feedback and business goals.

Focus: Delivery on time and budget
Key question: How will we deliver this work?
Success measure: On-time, on-budget delivery
Typical artefact: Project plan

Example: The project manager creates a timeline and coordinates tasks so the new quiz feature launches as scheduled.

Focus: Maximizing value from the development team
Key question: What’s the next most valuable thing to build?
Success measure: Value delivered in each sprint
Typical artefact: Prioritized backlog

Example: The product owner works with the team to clarify requirements and ensure the most important backlog items are ready for development.
